Gothic Horror · Barovia

Curse of Strahd

The mists have already decided you're not leaving. The only question is whether Count Strahd von Zarovich lets you live long enough to regret coming.

Your journey was supposed to end somewhere else. Then the fog rolled in — thick, wrong, alive — and swallowed the road behind you. When it finally thinned, you were somewhere it hadn't touched in a hundred years: the land of Barovia, and its master already knows your names.

Because in Barovia, nothing arrives by accident. The land itself chooses its guests. And its master has been waiting a very long time for new toys.

The World

Barovia is a demiplane of dread sealed off from the rest of existence by a wall of living mist. Its villages are grim, its people superstitious for good reason, and its castle looms over everything from a mountain that never quite lets you forget it's watching. Wolves don't behave like wolves here. Neither does the weather. Neither, most nights, does the dark.

The Villain

Count Strahd von Zarovich is not a monster who forgot he was once a man — he remembers everything, and that's worse. Centuries of undeath have sharpened his cruelty into something patient and theatrical. He does not simply want you dead. He wants to know exactly how you'll break first, and he has all the time in the world to find out.

A deck of fortune-telling cards, read by the wandering Vistani, will chart the only path anyone's ever found to defeating him — if you can survive long enough to walk it.

Why This Table

This is D&D's definitive gothic horror campaign, and I run it straight: dread over jump-scares, tragedy over slapstick, and a villain who remembers every choice you make. If you want candlelit dread, doomed romance, folk superstition, and a final showdown in a castle that's actively trying to kill you before Strahd does — welcome to Barovia.

Pack a torch. Distrust the fog. Barovia already knows you're coming.
